PRIORITY

Dr. R. Kim, PI, Imaging AI Lab
To: you · Re: ChestFind numbers before Friday

Thanks for cleaning the case files last week. The radiology department wants to know whether ChestFind, the pneumothorax detector the vendor is pitching, is good enough to switch on.

The vendor says it has 94% sensitivity. I have 240 of our own chest X-rays that two radiologists already read. Can you run ChestFind's scores against their reads and tell me what sensitivity we actually get, and whether it works equally well at both of our sites?

By the end you can

  • Build a confusion matrix from model scores and a threshold
  • Calculate sensitivity and specificity, and explain the trade-off between them
  • Compare performance between two sites (a subgroup analysis)

Other ways to frame the whole lab

    
The demo uses version A. Each of these could replace it; the map, unlock codes and puzzles work with all of them.

    

      
A · in the demo
The ChestFind audit
One fictional tool runs through every lane: clean its data, measure it, find where it fails, catch it reading chest tubes, check its draft reports, write the audit as the capstone abstract.
Strongest story; mirrors real vendor evaluation and the Leadership track.

      
B
Research rotation inbox
The lab page looks like an email inbox. Your PI, a statistician and a resident send requests ("can you make this figure for my RSNA poster?"). Each email is a mission; replies are the report-back answers.
Feels most like real research life; more writing to build.

      
C
Reproduce a real paper
Each lane recreates one figure from a published radiology AI paper using open data (a ROC curve, a saliency figure, a report-labeling table). Your figure is laid next to the published one.
Most "real"; depends on papers with open data and code.

      
D
Mr. Diaz, continued
Carries the core course's patient into the lab: his chest X-ray is in the dataset, and each mission asks what the AI would have done for him and patients like him.
Ties the tracks together; one patient may feel thin across 12 labs.

    


    
Missions worth adding

    

      
Data Science
Scrub the headers
DICOM files carry patient names, dates and IDs in their headers. Find and remove the protected health information before the data can leave the hospital. A missed field shows up as a "privacy breach" on the report-back.
Teaches de-identification, which every student project hits first.

      
Data Science
How many cases do we need?
Dr. Kim wants a validation study. Use a sample-size slider to find how many X-rays you need to estimate sensitivity within plus or minus 5%, then defend it.
Short; links to Leadership L3 (local validation).

      
Imaging AI
Label it yourself
Label 30 chest X-rays for pneumothorax, then see your agreement with two radiologists and with other learners (a kappa score). Shows why "ground truth" is messy.
Needs the shared database for the class comparison.

      
Imaging AI
Hospital B
Your model scores well at home. Then you get images from a second hospital with a different scanner, and performance drops. Find out why and fix it.
The clearest hands-on version of lesson 1.5.

      
Language
Prompt as protocol
Write a prompt that pulls "pneumothorax: yes or no" out of 100 reports. Your prompt is scored against gold labels; refine it and keep a version log, like a study protocol.
Needs an LLM key or a small local model; could run on precomputed outputs.

      
Foundations
Git rescue
Your notebook broke the night before a deadline. Use the commit history to find the last working version and restore it.
Makes version control feel useful rather than a chore.

    


    
More game mechanics to consider

    

      
Low effort
Bug hunts
"This pipeline puts the same patient in training and test. Find the line." Fewer hints used means a higher rating. Same engine as the puzzle in tab 3.

      
Low effort
Lane badges on the portfolio
Finishing a lane adds a badge to the learner's GitHub README, the kind of thing a mentor actually looks at.

      
Medium
Reviewer 2 boss level
End of each lane: a short flawed results paragraph to critique, then compare your review with an expert's. Unlocks the capstone.

      
Medium
Opt-in challenge board
Submit predictions on a held-out test set; a scoreboard ranks AUC. Opt-in so beginners aren't put off. Seasonal, e.g. one challenge per semester.

      
Would skip
Streaks and points
Daily streaks and leaderboards on everything reward clicking, not understanding, and clash with the course's tone. Badges tied to real work are enough.
