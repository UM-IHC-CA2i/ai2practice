Framework

Never-Skilling and Medical Students

As a medical student, you are in the earliest stages of building diagnostic skills. When AI provides findings for you in the reading room, you skip the cognitive effort required to develop pattern recognition. The result is false proficiency — you perform well with AI but have no independent capability.

In one study, students who had unrestricted access to an AI tutor scored 17% lower on subsequent unaided exams. The AI helped in the moment but prevented durable learning.

Mis-Skilling: Learning Wrong Patterns

When you learn alongside AI tools that have systematic errors, you can absorb those error patterns as if they were correct. For example, an AI tool that overcalls cardiomegaly on AP films can train you to overcall cardiomegaly on PA films as well.

Mis-skilling is especially dangerous for students because you are building foundational patterns for the first time. Unlike attendings who can recognize when AI contradicts their training, you may accept AI patterns as ground truth.

Why Deskilling Is Less Relevant for Students

Deskilling requires existing skills to erode. As a medical student with limited imaging experience, your primary risk is never building these skills in the first place (never-skilling) or building them incorrectly (mis-skilling).

Deskilling becomes relevant later in your career, particularly during residency and practice. For now, focus on ensuring you build independent competencies from the start.

The False Proficiency Trap

You are on your MS3 radiology elective, and the reading room has an AI overlay on every chest X-ray. It highlights a left pleural effusion with a 92% confidence label. You look at the image, see exactly what the AI told you to see, and present to your attending: "Left pleural effusion and cardiomegaly."

What you did not notice: a subtle right lower lobe consolidation (the AI missed it), mediastinal widening, and the fact that this was an AP portable. You did not miss these things because you are a bad student. You missed them because you were reading the AI's output, not the image.

Key Takeaway

Learning requires struggle. AI removes the struggle. You perform well in the moment but build nothing durable. When AI is removed, there is nothing underneath.

What You Can Do
  • Form your own impression first. Before looking at AI output, spend time with the image. Write down what you see, then check the AI.
  • Track your independent accuracy. Keep a log: your impression before AI vs. the final attending read. Watch for patterns of dependency.
  • Seek out uncertainty. The cases that make you unsure are building your skills. AI eliminates that uncertainty. Your training depends on it.
  • Learn what AI does not cover. Every AI tool has blind spots. See the AI Tool Tracker.
