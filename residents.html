Framework

Competency Risks Across Training

As a radiology resident, you face all three AI-related competency risks. Never-skilling threatens skills you have not yet built. Deskilling threatens skills you are actively developing — AI reliance can erode them before they are fully consolidated. Mis-skilling means absorbing AI error patterns into your own diagnostic reasoning.

In the Dratsch et al. mammography study, even experienced radiologists followed incorrect AI nearly half the time — accuracy dropped from 82% to 46%. For inexperienced readers, it fell as low as 20%. If you never build independent skills, you stay in the "inexperienced" category permanently.

Training Timeline: Three Phases

Ke et al. propose a three-phase progression from AI-free foundations to full collaboration:

Phase 1 — R1 to R2: AI-Free Foundational Competency

Build independent pattern recognition before AI exposure. Structured AI-free reading sessions. Demonstrate baseline competency before any AI integration.

Phase 2 — R2 to R3: Guided Integration

Learn when AI is right vs. wrong. Adversarial exercises with deliberately embedded errors. Build calibration — the ability to recognize AI mistakes.

Phase 3 — R4 to R5: Supervised Collaboration

Full AI integration with decision attribution. Periodic AI-free competency checks to prevent deskilling. Document when you agree with, override, or supplement AI.

Automation Bias

Automation bias is the tendency to uncritically accept AI output. It is an in-the-moment behavior — following what AI says without independently verifying. Over time, repeated automation bias leads to never-skilling (if you never develop independent skills) or deskilling (if existing skills atrophy from disuse).

The Pesapane et al. study showed that explainable AI with saliency heatmaps reduced automation bias from 36.1% to 17.8%. When AI shows why it reached a conclusion, readers engage more critically.

Practical Tips
  • Dictate before you check AI. Form your impression and start dictating before checking AI output. Even a brief mental formulation preserves independent reasoning.
  • Track your disagreements. When you disagree with AI, note it and check who was right. This builds calibration. If you never disagree, that is a warning sign.
  • Request AI-free reading sessions. Even one session per week preserves foundational skills.
  • Learn AI's blind spots. Every tool has pathologies it detects well and ones it misses. See the AI Tool Tracker.
  • Document decision attribution. Note when you agree with, override, or supplement AI findings. This creates accountability and reveals patterns.
