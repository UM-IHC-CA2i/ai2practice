Question 1 of 15
A medical student uses an AI overlay on every chest X-ray during their radiology elective. When asked to read a film without AI, they cannot identify a pleural effusion they have never independently searched for. This is:
Never-skilling. The student never built independent search patterns. AI did the cognitive work from the start. Deskilling would require existing skills to erode; the student never had them.
Question 2 of 15
A chest AI tool consistently overcalls cardiomegaly on AP portable films. After months of exposure, a student starts overcalling cardiomegaly on PA films without AI. This is:
Mis-skilling. The AI's systematic error has been internalized. The student now makes the same error independently, even without AI.
Question 3 of 15
The "calibration paradox" described by Ke et al. refers to:
To oversee AI, you need calibration — knowing when AI is right vs. wrong. But calibration develops through independent practice, the exact process AI disrupts.
Question 4 of 15
"False proficiency" in the context of AI-assisted training means:
False proficiency is the core problem of never-skilling. The trainee appears competent with AI, but their independent performance does not exist.
Question 5 of 15
Why is radiology particularly vulnerable to never-skilling compared to surgical specialties?
Radiology's core competency — visual pattern recognition and differential diagnosis — is precisely what diagnostic AI does. In surgery, motor skills resist automation.
Question 6 of 15
In the Keshavarz et al. systematic review (2026) of 14 AI education studies in radiology, how many tested AI-withdrawal effects?
Zero out of 14 studies tested what happens when AI is removed. All measured performance with AI, but none tested the durability of that performance.
Question 7 of 15
A student consistently agrees with AI's "normal" reading without independently reviewing the image. This in-the-moment behavior is:
Automation bias is uncritically accepting AI output in the moment. If this pattern continues, it may lead to never-skilling or mis-skilling.
Question 8 of 15
According to Ke et al.'s framework, trainees should encounter AI with deliberately embedded errors at which level?
Level 2 (Adversarial Calibration) exposes trainees to AI output with deliberate errors. They must detect and correct errors.
Question 9 of 15
Which strategy best protects against never-skilling?
The key is sequencing. Build independent skills first, then introduce AI. The correct approach is Ke's Phase 1: independent milestones before AI exposure.
Question 10 of 15
In the Dratsch et al. mammography study, when AI provided incorrect BI-RADS suggestions, experienced radiologists' accuracy dropped from approximately:
Experienced radiologists' accuracy dropped from 82% to 46% with incorrect AI. Inexperienced readers dropped as low as 20%.
Question 11 of 15
An AI tool detects 85% of nodules on chest CT. For a student who has only practiced reading with AI, the remaining 15% represents:
If you have only practiced where AI points, that 15% becomes invisible to you. You have never developed the search patterns to find what AI misses.
Question 12 of 15
Mis-skilling differs from automation bias in that:
Mis-skilling means the error patterns have been internalized — they persist independently of AI. Automation bias is an in-the-moment behavior.
Question 13 of 15
The best way to identify whether you are developing false proficiency is:
The only way to detect false proficiency is to test without AI. If your unassisted performance is significantly worse, you may have false proficiency.
Question 14 of 15
Why is deskilling less relevant for medical students than for attending radiologists?
Deskilling is the erosion of existing skills through disuse. Medical students have limited baseline imaging skills, so their primary risks are never-skilling and mis-skilling.
Question 15 of 15
The Pesapane et al. study found that automation bias was reduced from 36.1% to 17.8% by:
Explainable AI (XAI) with saliency-based heatmaps reduced automation bias from 36.1% to 17.8%. When AI shows why it reached a conclusion, readers engage more critically.
