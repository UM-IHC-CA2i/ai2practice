Radiology Basics: Images, Systems & Reports

Radiology from the ground up, for students with no radiology background: how images are made, what they are to a computer, how a scan moves through the hospital, and how to read the report. One patient’s story runs through all four lessons.

  • Core question: What is radiology, and how does a scan become data?
  • 4 lessons
  • 15-20 min each
  • No radiology background needed

Lessons

Each lesson follows Mr. Diaz, a patient with a suspected blood clot in the lungs, from the first scan to the report. Where AI plugs into this workflow is covered in Lesson 3.1.

  1. 2.1

    What Radiology Is

    Available

    The five main imaging tools, why things look white or black, slices and planes, and the radiation and contrast questions to ask before a scan.

  2. 2.2

    Images as Data

    Available

    Pixels and CT numbers, the DICOM file and its header, windowing, image quality and artifacts, and how de-identified images become AI datasets.

  3. 2.3

    The Journey of a Scan

    Available

    One patient’s CT from order to follow-up: the EHR, RIS and PACS, why the reason for exam matters, critical results, and where errors happen.

  4. 2.4

    Reading a Radiology Report

    Available

    The parts of a report, impression versus findings, hedging language, RADS categories, and explaining a report to a patient in plain words.
