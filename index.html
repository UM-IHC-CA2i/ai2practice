Three Ways AI Compromises Clinical Competency

Drawing from Ke et al.'s framework (Nature Medicine, 2026), three distinct mechanisms by which AI can undermine clinical skills.

Never-Skilling

Skills that were never built

Never-skilling is the failure to develop foundational competencies because AI substitutes for the cognitive effort required to build them. The skill was never built in the first place.

The result is false proficiency — AI-assisted performance appears adequate, but unaided performance is far below standard.

Radiology Example

An R1 uses AI nodule triage on every chest CT for six months. When the system goes down, she reports a CT as normal. Due to her dependence on the AI system, she never developed the skills to catch chest nodules in the first place.

Deskilling

Existing skills that erode from disuse

Deskilling is the erosion of existing competencies through disuse. An established skill weakens because prolonged AI reliance removes the practice needed to maintain it. Unlike never-skilling, the cognitive foundation exists but has been degraded.

Deskilling is more easily reversible with deliberate practice because the foundational pathways were already built; however, without intervention, the erosion continues.

Radiology Example

An attending mammographer has read independently for 15 years. After 2 years of AI-assisted CAD, her unassisted accuracy has dropped. Skills she once had — pattern recognition for subtle architectural distortion — have weakened from disuse.

Mis-Skilling

Wrong patterns learned from AI errors

Mis-skilling occurs when trainees develop incorrect patterns of thinking by repeatedly accepting AI errors without questioning them. Over time, these errors can become part of the trainee's own diagnostic reasoning, even when the AI is no longer being used.

Unlike automation bias, which occurs when a clinician accepts an AI recommendation in a specific moment, mis-skilling can persist after the AI is no longer present.

Radiology Example

A chest AI tool that consistently overcalls cardiomegaly on CXRs. After seeing this repeatedly, a resident begins to overcall cardiomegaly on PA films as well. When reviewing future CXRs without AI, the resident still overcalls cardiomegaly. The AI's error has influenced the trainee's own interpretation.
