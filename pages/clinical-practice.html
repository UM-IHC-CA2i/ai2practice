02 / Clinical Practice

Clinical Practice

Evaluate AI as part of a clinical system—not as an isolated model. Focus on evidence quality, local applicability, workflow, human–AI interaction, and clinical consequences.

OverviewEvidence appraisalHuman–AI interactionCases & assessmentsNext step

Evaluate the human–AI system

Clinical Practice shifts the unit of analysis from the model alone to the model embedded in care. A strong algorithm can still produce weak clinical value if the evidence does not transfer, the workflow is poorly designed, or users over-rely on its output.

Core question: Does this AI improve the clinical task, for these users, in this workflow, under the conditions in which it will actually be used?

Evidence appraisal

Read clinical AI studies with attention to the target population, reference standard, comparator, operating point, external validation, prospective testing, and the outcomes that were actually measured.

Lens 01

Validity

Was the evaluation designed to answer the clinical question being claimed?

Lens 02

Transferability

How closely do the study population, data-generating process, and workflow match the intended environment?

Lens 03

Clinical utility

Does the intervention change decisions, efficiency, outcomes, or another endpoint that matters?

Lens 04

Failure visibility

Can users recognize when the system is uncertain, wrong, or operating outside its expected conditions?

Human–AI interaction

Clinical performance emerges from interaction: interface design, timing, task allocation, user expertise, trust, reliance, and the cost of both missed assistance and inappropriate deference.

Questions to ask

  • When does the AI output appear in the workflow?
  • What action is the user expected to take, and what happens when they disagree?
  • Can reliance be measured rather than inferred from satisfaction?
  • Are automation bias and alert fatigue plausible failure modes?

From evaluation to institutional decision-making

When a tool appears clinically promising, the next task is operational: selecting the right product, validating it locally, defining ownership, and monitoring it after deployment.
