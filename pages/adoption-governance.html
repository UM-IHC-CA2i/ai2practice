Leadership

A role-specific pathway for people leading clinical AI: strategy and clinical need, evidence and local validation, implementation and change, governance and monitoring, workforce readiness, and the technical infrastructure that supports safe use.

Lead AI from need to sustained clinical value

Leadership is broader than procurement or governance alone. It connects clinical strategy, evidence, local validation, workflow and change management, technical infrastructure, education, accountability, and post-deployment learning.

Core question: How should a clinical team evaluate, deploy, and steward AI responsibly over time?

The AI lifecycle in practice

Stage 1

Define the need

What problem are we actually trying to solve?

Specify the clinical problem, target users, intended workflow, baseline performance, and measurable success criteria before looking at vendors.

Stage 2

Select & procure

Which tool best fits that need?

Evaluate published evidence, intended use, interoperability, security, workflow fit, cost, vendor claims, and contractual responsibilities.

Stage 3

Validate locally

Does it work here?

Assess performance and operational fit on local data or in a controlled prospective workflow before routine deployment.

Stage 4

Implement

How will people use it safely?

Define rollout, user training, escalation, accountability, change management, and the fallback path when the system is unavailable or unreliable.

Stage 5

Monitor & govern

Is it still working, safe, and worth using?

Track reliability, subgroup performance, drift, incidents, downstream outcomes, utilization, and whether the tool continues to provide value.

Leadership domains

Strategy

Clinical need & value

Problem definition, baseline workflow, success criteria, economics, and deciding whether AI is the right intervention.

Evidence

Selection & local validation

Published evidence, intended use, local data, subgroup performance, workflow fit, and acceptance criteria.

Operations

Implementation & change

Integration, rollout, training, escalation, downtime, human factors, and ownership of clinical decisions.

Stewardship

Governance & monitoring

Performance, drift, incidents, change control, accountability, utilization, and continued clinical value.

People

Education & workforce

Competency, role design, communication, feedback loops, and preparing users for effective human-AI collaboration.

Governance is an operating system, not a committee name

Effective governance connects technical monitoring to clinical ownership and decision rights. The practical questions are who reviews signals, what thresholds trigger action, who can pause a system, how incidents are investigated, and how changes are documented.

Minimum governance domains

  • Clinical ownership and accountability
  • Technical performance and data-quality monitoring
  • Safety-event review and escalation
  • Change control and version tracking
  • Periodic reassessment of clinical value
