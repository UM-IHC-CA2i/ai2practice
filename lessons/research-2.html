Key Concepts

Descriptive Statistics — Numerical summaries of data. Central tendency (mean, median, mode) tells you the typical value. Spread (standard deviation, IQR) tells you how much values vary. These appear in the "Table 1" of every research paper.

t-test — A statistical test that compares the means of two groups and determines whether the difference is statistically significant. Used when comparing continuous measurements (e.g., AI confidence scores) between two conditions.

p-value — The probability of observing a difference as large as (or larger than) the one measured, assuming there is truly no difference. By convention, p < 0.05 is considered statistically significant. A small p-value does not tell you the size or clinical importance of the effect.

Seaborn — A Python visualization library built on matplotlib that produces publication-quality statistical graphics. Supports histograms, box plots, violin plots, heatmaps, regression plots, and pair plots with minimal code.

Confusion Matrix — A 2x2 table that categorizes every AI prediction into four outcomes: True Positive, False Positive, False Negative, True Negative. All evaluation metrics (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV) are derived from these four numbers.

Sensitivity and Specificity — Sensitivity = TP / (TP + FN), the proportion of real findings the AI caught. Specificity = TN / (TN + FP), the proportion of normal studies correctly identified. These trade off against each other as the confidence threshold changes.

ROC Curve and AUC — The ROC curve plots sensitivity against (1 - specificity) at every threshold. AUC (Area Under the Curve) summarizes overall discriminative performance: 1.0 is perfect, 0.5 is random. AUC is the most common metric in radiology AI papers.

Subgroup Analysis — Evaluating AI performance separately for different modalities, body regions, or populations. Overall accuracy can mask significant disparities. FDA guidance increasingly requires subgroup reporting for AI diagnostic tools.
