Understanding Risk Levels

High = Finding-level diagnoses (answer-delivery). AI provides specific findings. Highest never-skilling risk because the trainee's cognitive work is replaced.

Medium = Triage or limited findings. AI handles detection/prioritization but the trainee still interprets. Moderate risk.

Low = Binary classification (normal vs. abnormal). The trainee must identify all specific findings. Lowest risk to independent skill development.

Risk Mapping

Highest risk: pathologies where AI is good enough to replace search but not good enough to catch everything.

Highest Risk: AI is good but imperfect

Lung nodules, subtle pneumothorax, small consolidations. AI detects most (80–95%) so trainees stop searching independently, but it misses the subtle cases that require independent skill. The gap between AI's good-enough performance and 100% is where never-skilling creates real patient harm.

Moderate Risk: AI creates miscalibration

Cardiomegaly, rib fractures, effusions. AI is generally good but has systematic biases (e.g., overcalling cardiomegaly on AP films, false-positive fractures at costochondral junctions). Primary risk: mis-skilling from absorbed errors.

Lower Risk: AI has limited coverage

Mediastinal abnormalities, bone lesions, soft tissue. Trainees must maintain independent assessment here. However, there is a risk of false completeness — believing a clean AI report means a normal study when AI simply does not evaluate these areas.

Key Questions About Your Department's AI

What pathologies does the AI detect?

Every AI tool has a defined detection list. Anything not on it is your responsibility alone. Know the boundaries of what your department's AI covers.

What is the false-negative rate for subtle findings?

Published sensitivity often reflects clear-cut cases. Subtle findings — small nodules, early architectural distortion, tiny pneumothoraces — may have significantly lower detection rates.

Triage mode or answer-delivery mode?

Triage preserves more of the trainee's diagnostic reasoning. Answer-delivery replaces more cognitive work. Know which mode your department's AI operates in — this determines the never-skilling risk level.

Any known false-positive patterns?

Systematic false positives create mis-skilling risk. If the AI consistently overcalls certain findings, trainees may internalize those error patterns. Ask your attendings about known biases in your department's AI tools.
